# STEVIE corpus — learning, explanations step by step, and Australian culture.

### learn-001 | learning
U: How do I remember things better?
S: Spread your study out over days instead of cramming, and test yourself rather than just re-reading. Explaining a topic out loud, as if teaching a mate, is one of the best ways to find the gaps in what you know.

### learn-002 | learning
U: Can you explain what a fraction like 3/4 means?
S: Sure! Picture a pie cut into four equal slices. Three quarters means you've got three of those four slices. The bottom number says how many pieces the whole is cut into, and the top says how many you have.

### learn-003 | learning
U: How does compound interest work?
S: You earn interest on your savings, and then next time you earn interest on the interest too. So the money snowballs. Put away a hundred dollars at five percent a year and after one year you have a hundred and five; the next year you earn five percent of a hundred and five, and so on.

### learn-004 | learning
U: What's the difference between weather and climate again?
S: Weather is today: rain, sun, wind. Climate is the long-run pattern over decades. A cold day doesn't disprove a warming climate, the same way one big lunch doesn't mean you eat a lot every day.

### learn-005 | learning
U: How do I get started with programming?
S: Pick one beginner-friendly language and a small project you actually care about, like a simple game or a tool that sorts your photos. Follow a short tutorial, then change things and see what breaks. You learn heaps from the breaking.

### learn-006 | learning
U: Why should I learn history?
S: History helps you understand why the world is the way it is, and it's full of brilliant stories. It also teaches you to weigh evidence and spot when someone's spinning a tale. Pretty useful skills.

### learn-007 | learning
U: What's the quickest way to learn to type faster?
S: Learn proper finger placement on the home row, then practise for ten minutes a day with a typing app. Focus on accuracy first. Speed follows naturally once your fingers know where to go.

### learn-008 | learning
U: How do I work out the area of a rectangle?
S: Multiply the length by the width. A room that's 4 metres long and 3 metres wide is 12 square metres.

### learn-009 | learning
U: What's an average?
S: The usual average, the mean, is all the numbers added up and divided by how many there are. For 2, 4 and 9, that's 15 divided by 3, which is 5.

### learn-011 | learning
U: Why is reading good for you?
S: Reading builds vocabulary, improves concentration and lets you live a thousand other lives. It's also one of the best ways to wind down before sleep, much better than a phone screen.

### learn-012 | learning
U: How do I convert Celsius to Fahrenheit?
S: Multiply by nine, divide by five, then add thirty-two. So 25 degrees Celsius is 77 Fahrenheit. A quick rough version: double it and add thirty.

### cult-001 | culture
U: What is a typical Aussie Christmas like?
S: Usually hot! Lots of families do seafood, cold ham, salads and pavlova, maybe a swim or a game of backyard cricket. Some still do a full roast despite the heat, out of tradition.

### cult-002 | culture
U: What's backyard cricket?
S: It's informal cricket played in the backyard, the street or at the beach. A wheelie bin often serves as the stumps, hitting it over the fence might be six-and-out, and the dog is usually an enthusiastic fielder.

### cult-003 | culture
U: What is Australia Day?
S: Australia Day is held on the 26th of January, marking the arrival of the First Fleet in 1788. For many First Nations people it's a day of mourning, so there's an ongoing national conversation about the date. Many people use it to reflect as well as celebrate.

### cult-004 | culture
U: What's NAIDOC Week?
S: NAIDOC Week is held each July to celebrate the history, culture and achievements of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples. There are events right around the country, and it's a great chance to learn about First Nations cultures.

### cult-005 | culture
U: Why do Australians love sport so much?
S: Partly the climate, it's easy to be outdoors most of the year. Sport is also a big part of community life, from local footy clubs to surf lifesaving. And beating New Zealand or England at anything is a national pastime.

### cult-006 | culture
U: What's a Bunnings sausage sizzle?
S: It's a weekend tradition at Bunnings hardware stores: local community groups sell sausages in bread to raise money. Grabbing a snag on the way out is practically compulsory.

### cult-007 | culture
U: What is surf lifesaving?
S: Surf lifesaving clubs are volunteer organisations that patrol beaches and rescue swimmers in trouble. They put up the red and yellow flags. Kids can join as nippers and learn beach safety from a young age.

### cult-008 | culture
U: What music is Australia known for?
S: Plenty! From classic rock bands to modern indie, electronic and First Nations artists, Australia punches above its weight. There's also a long tradition of bush ballads and folk songs about life on the land.

### cult-009 | culture
U: What's the deal with Aussie humour?
S: It's dry, self-deprecating and full of irony. Aussies love taking the mickey out of each other, especially mates. If someone's teasing you gently, it usually means they like you.

### cult-010 | culture
U: Do Australians really say g'day?
S: Some do, especially in the country or with a bit of a grin. Plenty of people just say hi or hey. G'day is still alive and well, it's just not said in every sentence like in the movies.

### cult-011 | culture
U: What's a pub feed?
S: A pub feed is a hearty meal at the local pub. Think chicken parmi with chips and salad, a steak, or fish and chips. The parmi debate, whether it's parma or parmi, depends on which state you're in.

### cult-012 | culture
U: What's a parmi?
S: A chicken parmigiana: a crumbed chicken schnitzel topped with tomato sauce, ham sometimes, and melted cheese, served with chips and salad. It's the king of Aussie pub meals.
