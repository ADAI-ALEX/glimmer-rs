# Datasets: sources, licences and provenance

STEVIE is trained in two stages, each on its own data:

1. **Pretraining** teaches general English: vocabulary, grammar, sentence structure and some world
   knowledge. Data: a mixture of three openly licensed corpora (below), 137M training tokens.
2. **Supervised fine-tuning** teaches the conversation format and STEVIE's personality. Data: an
   original corpus of 687 Australian-English conversations written for this project.

Nothing is downloaded that is not declared in [`datasets/sources/`](../datasets/sources), and no
third-party data is redistributed by this repository: `glimmer data fetch` downloads it from the
original hosts, and every build records what was used.

## Pretraining sources

| source | licence | what it contributes | raw → kept documents | characters | training tokens |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [TinyStories V2 (GPT-4 subset)](https://huggingface.co/datasets/roneneldan/TinyStories) | CDLA-Sharing-1.0 | fluent, simple narrative English; designed for 1–30M-parameter models | 305,373 → 305,357 | 245.1M | 60.8M |
| [Simple English Wikipedia](https://huggingface.co/datasets/wikimedia/wikipedia) (2023-11-01) | CC BY-SA 3.0 / GFDL | broad expository vocabulary and world knowledge in plain English | 241,787 → 191,864 | 233.1M | 70.7M |
| [Australian public-domain literature](https://www.gutenberg.org) (28 books) | Public domain (AU & US) | authentic Australian English: bush life, vernacular dialogue, slang verse | 3,925 → 3,913 chunks | 10.3M | 5.6M (×2 upsampled) |

**Licence notes.**

* *TinyStories* — synthetic stories generated by GPT-4 and released by Eldan & Li (Microsoft
  Research) under CDLA-Sharing-1.0, which permits use and sharing. Model outputs are "Results"
  under the licence and are not restricted. Only the first 250 MB of the 2.2 GB file is
  downloaded (HTTP range request); the incomplete final story is discarded.
* *Simple English Wikipedia* — share-alike text. GLIMMER does not redistribute it; attribution is
  recorded in every dataset manifest built from it.
* *Australian literature* — every author died before 1955 (public domain in Australia) and every
  work was published before 1929 (public domain in the United States). The Project Gutenberg
  licence governs only the "Project Gutenberg" trademark and boilerplate, which is stripped. Each
  book is recorded with its author's year of death in
  [`gutenberg-au.toml`](../datasets/sources/gutenberg-au.toml).

The Gutenberg selection: A. B. "Banjo" Paterson (5 books incl. *The Man from Snowy River* and
*The Old Bush Songs*), Henry Lawson (7, incl. *While the Billy Boils*), C. J. Dennis (*The Songs
of a Sentimental Bloke*, *Digger Smith*, *A Book for Kids*), Steele Rudd (*On Our Selection*),
Miles Franklin (*My Brilliant Career*, *Some Everyday Folk and Dawn*), Joseph Furphy (*Such Is
Life*), Rolf Boldrewood, Barbara Baynton, Ada Cambridge, Ernest Favenc, Louis Becke, Catherine
Helen Spence and Henry Kendall.

### Mixture and splits

| split | documents | tokens |
|---|---|---|
| train | 500,079 | 137,135,740 |
| val | 2,489 | 664,704 |
| test | 2,479 | 715,694 |

The literature corpus is upsampled ×2 so that Australian English is a meaningful share of what
the model reads (≈ 8% of training tokens). Splits are assigned by a hash of each document's id
(0.5% validation, 0.5% test), *before* upsampling, so copies of a document never straddle splits.

## Processing pipeline

```text
fetch    → raw bytes + SHA-256 + URL + timestamp            (data/raw/<source>/provenance.json)
prepare  → normalise → filter → dedup → JSONL                (data/corpora/<source>.jsonl + stats)
tokenizer→ BPE on the training split only                     (data/tokenizers/glimmer-8k.json)
build    → tokenise → split → shuffle → u16 token files       (data/datasets/<name>/ + manifest.json)
```

1. **Normalisation**: Unicode NFC; curly quotes, non-breaking spaces and CRLF unified; control and
   zero-width characters removed; trailing whitespace trimmed; blank-line runs collapsed.
2. **Gutenberg clean-up**: header/footer removed at the `*** START/END OF` markers; transcriber
   notes and boilerplate paragraphs dropped; hard-wrapped prose unwrapped while verse keeps its
   line breaks (a paragraph whose lines are mostly short is treated as verse); books chunked into
   ~3,000-character documents on paragraph boundaries.
3. **Quality filters**: minimum/maximum length and a minimum fraction of alphabetic characters.
   Simple Wikipedia loses 45,937 stub articles under 200 characters and 3,784 list-like pages.
4. **Offensive-language filter**: some 19th-century texts contain racial slurs. Paragraphs
   containing a blocklisted term are dropped (not edited). The list lives in
   [`datasets/filters/blocklist.rot13`](../datasets/filters/blocklist.rot13), ROT13-encoded so the
   terms aren't displayed while browsing. It touched 177 literature chunks and 161 Wikipedia
   articles. Period texts still carry dated attitudes in subtler ways; they are a small minority of
   the mixture and the fine-tuned persona is defined by the original corpus.
5. **Deduplication**: exact (FNV-1a of case/whitespace-normalised text) for every source; MinHash
   near-duplicate removal (128 hashes, word shingles, LSH banding) where enabled.
6. **Tokenisation** with the project's own byte-level BPE (8,192 tokens), trained on a balanced
   90 MB sample of the *training split* of each corpus.

## The STEVIE conversation corpus

See [`datasets/stevie/README.md`](../datasets/stevie/README.md) for the full provenance statement.

| | conversations | messages | tokens | supervised tokens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| train | 628 | 1,716 | 45,862 | 32,469 |
| val | 34 | 100 | 2,737 | 1,925 |
| test | 25 | 64 | 1,901 | 1,367 |

* **Origin**: written by hand for GLIMMER, conversation by conversation, by Claude (Anthropic)
  acting as the project's engineer in November 2026. Not derived from any existing dataset,
  transcript or person. Released under Apache-2.0 with the code.
* **Coverage**: 21 topic files — greetings, identity and limitations, small talk, emotional
  support, wildlife and conservation (two files), food, Australian geography and culture, slang
  explained, technology and Rust, science and maths, advice, humour, nature and travel, general
  knowledge, quick-fire casual replies, opinions and problem-solving, learning and culture, and
  three sets of multi-turn conversations where replies depend on earlier context (names, pets,
  plans, places).
* **Character**: STEVIE is a fictional AI whose name nods to the spirit of Australian wildlife
  enthusiasm. The corpus deliberately avoids the catchphrases, voice or identity of any real person
  (corpus QC warns on impersonation markers such as "Crikey") and STEVIE says plainly that it is an
  AI, not affiliated with anyone.
* **Quality control**: structural validation (alternating turns, starts with the user, ends with
  STEVIE, unique ids), length limits, exact and MinHash near-duplicate detection (threshold 0.7).
* **Leakage control**: every training conversation is compared with the
  [held-out evaluation prompts](../datasets/stevie/eval_prompts.toml); overlaps are excluded
  automatically. During development the check caught one conversation whose question matched an
  evaluation prompt word for word; it was rewritten. The four example exchanges in the project
  brief are evaluation prompts, deliberately absent from training.
* **Format for training**: the chat template `<|system|>…<|end|><|user|>…<|end|><|assistant|>…<|end|>`;
  half of the examples carry the short system prompt "You are Stevie, a friendly Australian AI
  mate." so the model works with or without it. Loss is computed only on STEVIE's tokens and the
  `<|end|>` that closes each of its turns.

## Reproducing

```bash
glimmer data pipeline     # fetch → prepare → tokenizer → build all datasets
glimmer datasets inspect pretrain-mix
glimmer datasets inspect stevie-sft
glimmer sources           # licence and status of every source
```

Downloads are checksummed and recorded; split assignment, upsampling and shuffling are seeded, so
the same sources produce the same datasets.
